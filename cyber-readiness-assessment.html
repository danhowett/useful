Methodology

The standards behind this assessment

Every control in this assessment is cross-mapped to four recognised frameworks, so a finding can be defended to an auditor, a board, or a regulator on standards-based grounds, rather than organisational judgement alone.

Lifecycle basis: domain structure follows the PICERL incident lifecycle (Preparation → Identification → Containment → Eradication → Recovery → Lessons Learned) and references MITRE ATT&CK for detection coverage, alongside UK-specific regulatory language (ICO/UK GDPR 72-hour rule, NIS2, FCA/PRA) where relevant. Framework tagging: each of the 40 questions below carries a tag for NIST CSF 2.0, NCSC CAF, ISO/IEC 27035, and CIS Controls v8.1 — hover any tag in a domain view for the full reference. Where a framework simply has no equivalent control (shown as "—"), that's a genuine coverage gap worth knowing about, not an oversight in the tagging.
How scores are calculated: each control is scored 1–5 (Not In Place → Optimised & Evidenced). A domain's score is the weighted average of its answered controls only — unanswered controls are excluded, not counted as zero — where weight (1 or 2) reflects how foundational that control is within the domain. The overall readiness score is a plain average of the eight domain scores, so every domain counts equally regardless of how many controls it contains. Gap priority is fixed per control at design time, not derived from the score: a control becomes a gap once scored at or below its own threshold (usually ≤3), and its priority (P1/P2/P3, mapping to the 30/60/90-day roadmap phases) reflects how critical that control is to incident response — not how low it scored.

NIST CSF 2.0

nist.gov/cyberframeworkcsf.tools

The de-facto international baseline. Six functions — Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover — organise every control in this tool. Paired with SP 800-61 Rev. 3 (April 2025), NIST's own incident-response profile written specifically against CSF 2.0, which replaced the older Preparation/Detection/Containment phase model with function-aligned guidance.

UK NCSC CAF v4.0

ncsc.gov.uk/collection/cyber-assessment-framework

Outcome-based, and the natural anchor given this tool's ICO/FCA/NIS2 references. Four objectives — A: Managing Security Risk, B: Protecting Against Cyber Attacks, C: Detecting Cyber Security Events, D: Minimising Impact of Incidents — with Objective D mapping almost one-to-one onto this tool's own IR-specific domains.

ISO/IEC 27035:2023

Part 1 · iso.org/78973Part 2 · iso.org/78974

The auditable, certifiable route if a client or contract requires ISO alignment. Five phases — Plan & Prepare, Detect & Report, Assess & Decide, Respond & Recover, Learn Lessons — map cleanly onto PICERL, consistent with the phase language used elsewhere in this tool.

CIS Controls v8.1

cisecurity.org/controls/v8-118 controls

The most prescriptive of the four — useful for turning a maturity score into a specific technical safeguard. Control 17 (Incident Response Management) is the anchor for most Tier 1/2 domains; Controls 5, 6, 8, 11, 12 & 13 cover identity, logging, backup, and network response.
